Git Game vs Bot Two developers, one main. Most lines with the least blame at the release wins. Prototype — every new game is a fresh shuffle.

main

The rules, short

  • Each turn: draw 2, spend 3 ops. Unused ops are lost.
  • add puts hand cards on your mat; commit turns the mat into one commit; push puts your commits on main.
  • You can't push if you're behind. If the bot pushed since your pointer last moved, pull first (1 op, merge token) or pull --rebase (2 ops, clean).
  • Pulling a commit that touches the same file as one of yours is a conflict: keep yours (they lose it, you take a grudge: −1), keep theirs, or pay 1 op to keep both.
  • Commits on main are face-down: everyone sees the file and lines (they were announced), nobody sees whether it's a bug.
  • One card in five is a bug. Only you see yours. Push it and hope. blame flips a commit: a bug costs its author 3.
  • push --force erases everything after your pointer. Take a sin. A reflog in the victim's hand undoes it for free.
  • At 10 commits anyone can tag v1.0; the game also ends after 12 rounds. Every card is flipped; each bug costs 3.

Score

  • + lines of your clean commits on main
  • +1 per revert you played
  • −3 per bug of yours flipped, −1 per merge token, −1 per grudge against you, −1 per sin
  • 4 or more bugs in production: the release fails and the least blame wins
